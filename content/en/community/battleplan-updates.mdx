## Inside the Launch Build: What Future Battleplan Updates Would Build On

To judge any Battleplan updates fairly, you need to know what the base game is trying to do. This is a WW2 real-time strategy game built around drawing orders directly onto a map rather than clicking individual units into position. You sketch multi-stage offensives — preparatory bombardments, set-piece attacks, flanking splits — and then watch your officers execute them.

The command structure is modeled all the way down: headquarters, regiments, battalions, and companies. Infantry, tanks, engineers, anti-tank guns, artillery, and scouts each behave semi-autonomously. You can merge divisions into corps or split them down to company level, requisition and upgrade units mid-campaign, and hire or fire officers. Keep an officer alive and he gains veterancy — and quirks.

Battles play out over thousands of kilometers of terrain modeled from real-world geographic data. Roads, towns, forests, and swamps sit where they actually sit. Missions simulate second by second, run anywhere from one week to a month of in-game time, and let you pause, play live, or fast-forward.

A few habits make this easier:

- **Screenshot your settings and version before patching.** If something feels worse after an update, you'll have a reference point.
- **Read patch notes before loading a saved campaign.** Balance changes can invalidate a plan you spent an hour drawing.
- **Report bugs in the community hub rather than only in Discord chatter.** Developers triage what they can find and reproduce.
- **Don't assume silence means abandonment.** For a $29.99 strategy title, the first meaningful patch often lands weeks after launch, not hours.
