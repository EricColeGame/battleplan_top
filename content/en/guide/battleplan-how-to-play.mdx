It is a WW2 real-time strategy game from Foolish Mortals Games Inc., published by Slitherine Ltd. ## Battleplan Basics: What Kind of Strategy Game Is This?

Battleplan simulates enormous, month-long engagements with hundreds of thousands of troops, resolved second by second. You give high-level orders, and companies, battalions, and regiments act on their own initiative within the boundaries you set.

That design choice is the single most important thing to understand before you play. If you expect to drag individual squads around like in a classic action RTS, you will fight the game the whole way. Here is the quick reference sheet:

## Battleplan How to Play: The Core Loop, Step by Step

Every mission follows roughly the same rhythm. Internalize this loop and you will stop feeling lost.

| Step | What You Do | Why It Matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Read the briefing | Identify objectives, deadline, and friendly forces | Missions run for days or weeks, so time is a resource |
| 2. Study the terrain | Inspect roads, towns, forests, swamps, and elevation | Terrain dictates both movement speed and supply flow |
| 3. Build the order of battle | Assign divisions, combine into corps, split down to companies | The right mix beats raw numbers |
| 4. Requisition and upgrade | Spend resources on the units you actually need | Reinforcements are limited; waste is permanent |
| 5. Draw the plan | Sketch phases, axes of advance, and defensive lines on the map | This is your real command interface |
| 6. Assign and execute | Attach units to phases, then start time | Officers act autonomously inside your plan |
| 7. Adjust mid-mission | Pause, redraw, reinforce, or pull back | Plans survive contact only if you revise them |

The most common early error is skipping steps 2 and 3 and jumping straight to drawing arrows. A beautiful plan executed by an under-supplied division with no anti-tank guns will collapse the moment enemy armor appears.

## Drawing Orders on the Map: The Skill You Must Master

Drawing is the core verb of the game. Instead of clicking unit abilities, you literally sketch your intentions onto real-world geography — and the game interprets those strokes as multi-step, multi-unit orders.

**Multi-stage offensives.** Break an attack into phases: bombardment, breach, exploitation, consolidation. Splitting a division to capture several objectives at once is often better than a single heavy push down one road.

**Preparatory bombardments.** Set-piece attacks work best when artillery softens the target first. Place guns on high ground identified from real elevation data, then time the barrage to lift just as infantry step off.

**Defense in depth, counterattacks, and planned withdrawals.** Not every plan is an advance. Layered defensive lines, a staged withdrawal to a stronger position, and a counterattack onto an overextended enemy are all drawable orders — and often the difference between holding and collapsing.

| Plan Type | Best Used When | Watch Out For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Set-piece assault | You have artillery superiority and a narrow front | Predictable timing; enemy reserves |
| Defense in depth | Outnumbered, or buying time for reinforcements | Supply lines running through the defensive belt |
| Planned withdrawal | Your flank is exposed and supply is thin | Retreat routes that cross open ground |
| Counterattack | The enemy has overextended past their supply | Committing reserves too early |

## Chain of Command, Officers, and Unit Management

Battleplan models each division realistically: headquarters, regiments, battalions, and companies, all with distinct roles. You customize the entire chain of command and order of battle for your army.

| Unit Type | Primary Role | Practical Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infantry | Holding ground and assaulting towns | Needs artillery support before attacking |
| Tanks | Breakthrough and exploitation | Keep them out of dense terrain without infantry |
| Engineers | Breaching obstacles and fortifications | Essential for prepared enemy positions |
| Anti-tank guns | Stopping armored counterattacks | Place on likely enemy armor avenues |
| Artillery | Preparation, interdiction, counter-battery | High ground improves reach and effect |
| Scouts | Reconnaissance and screening | Cheap; use them before you commit |
| Headquarters | Coordination and order flow | Lose it and the formation gets clumsy |

Officers are the hidden layer of Battleplan how to play. You hire and fire them, and you can listen to their advice or ignore it. Keep an officer alive and they gain veterancy — along with quirks that may or may not help you. Losing experienced commanders mid-campaign hurts more than losing hardware, because replacements start green.

## Logistics, Supply, and Controlling Time

Supply is the mechanic that separates veterans from beginners. Units that run dry stop advancing, stop defending effectively, and eventually stop being an army.

- **Cut enemy supply lines** to starve formations without destroying them outright.
- **Capture supply depots** to fuel your own advance and extend your operational reach.
- **Pull back when supplies run low** rather than pushing into a collapse you cannot reverse.
- **Respect terrain:** rivers, swamps, and bad roads slow the flow of supply, so a "short" route may be the worst one.

| Time Control | When to Use It |
| --- | --- |
| Pause | Reading terrain, drawing complex multi-phase plans, reacting to a breakthrough |
| Real time | Watching an advance unfold and spotting friction points |
| Fast-forward | Quiet periods, rear-area build-up, waiting out bombardments |

Because missions can span a week to a month of simulated time, fast-forward is not cheating — it is a tool. But never fast-forward through an active engagement; that is exactly when you want to pause and redraw.

## Beginner Tips, Common Mistakes, and Performance

A few habits will improve your first campaign dramatically.

| Mistake | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Micromanaging companies | Give intent, not instructions; let officers execute |
| Ignoring supply until units stall | Check supply before every phase, not after |
| Attacking across open ground | Use forests, towns, and elevation for cover |
| One giant push on one axis | Split objectives and attack in phases |
| Discarding officer advice blindly | Their warnings often reflect real conditions on the ground |
| Neglecting anti-tank defense | Armor counterattacks end careless advances |

On the technical side, Battleplan is not demanding. Either way you need 8 GB of storage, DirectX 11, and a broadband connection. Interface text is available in English, French, German, Spanish, Polish, and Russian, with English full audio.

One last tip worth repeating: use the geo-tagged historical photos and videos scattered across the map. They are placed where they were actually taken, and they turn a tough mission into a history lesson.

## FAQ

**What is the fastest way to learn Battleplan how to play?**
Play the smallest available scenario first, pause constantly, and focus on one skill at a time — supply management, then defensive positioning, then multi-phase attacks. The game rewards deliberate thinking far more than speed.

**Can I pause during a mission?**
Yes. Every mission can be paused, played in real time, or fast-forwarded at will, so you are never forced to make a decision under a timer.

**How long does a single mission take?**
Mission lengths range from roughly one week to a month of simulated time, which is why logistics and planning matter so much more than reflexes.

**Is Battleplan multiplayer?**
It ships as a single-player experience with Steam Achievements, Steam Cloud, and Family Sharing. You can compare progress and approaches with other players, but the campaign itself is a solo command challenge.

Master the drawing tools, respect supply, and trust your officers to do their jobs — that is the whole of Battleplan how to play in one sentence. Everything else is practice.
