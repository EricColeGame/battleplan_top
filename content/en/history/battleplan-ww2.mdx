## What Is Battleplan WW2? Inside Slitherine's New Historical RTS

Battleplan is a historical World War II real-time strategy game developed by Foolish Mortals Games Inc. and published by Slitherine Ltd., the studio behind a long line of wargames aimed at players who care about simulation depth. It launched on October 12, 2026, at $29.99 USD.

The core pitch is unusual: you take command of entire divisions and fight through some of the war's largest battles, simulated second by second across missions that can run from one week to a full month of in-game time. You give high-level orders, then watch your officers and companies carry them out autonomously. You are a corps commander, not a squad leader.

## The Drawing-Board Command System

The signature mechanic of Battleplan WW2 is that you plan by drawing. Rather than issuing a single move order, you sketch multi-step, multi-unit plans directly onto the map — arrows, phases, objectives, and fallback lines. The developer describes this as intuitive, and the intent is clearly to mirror how staff officers actually thought: in stages, with contingencies.

That matters because the plan types the game supports are the classic operational tools of the war.

| Plan type | When to use it | What it accomplishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparatory bombardment | Before a large set-piece attack | Softens defenders and disrupts cohesion before infantry goes in |
| Defense in depth | When outnumbered or holding a long line | Trades space for time and absorbs breakthroughs |
| Counterattack | After an enemy push stalls | Recovers ground and punishes overextended units |
| Planned withdrawal | When supply is running thin | Preserves formations for a later offensive |
| Multi-objective split | When several towns must fall | Splits a division to take multiple points at once |
| Supply-line cut | When enemy flanks are exposed | Starves the enemy of fuel and ammunition |

The practical takeaway for new players is to plan in phases. One giant arrow across the map rarely survives contact with a defended town. Three smaller phases — bombardment, breach, exploit — usually do.

## Order of Battle: Officers, Units, and Veterancy

Battleplan WW2 models each division with a real internal structure: headquarters, regiments, battalions, and companies. That structure is not decorative. You customize the chain of command and the order of battle for your entire army, combining divisions into corps or splitting them down to company level.

You can requisition and upgrade units throughout a campaign, and you can hire and fire the officers who lead your divisions. Officers offer advice, which you are free to take or ignore. Keeping an officer alive matters because survivors accumulate veterancy over time — along with quirks that may or may not help you.

| Unit type | Primary job | Pairs well with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infantry | Hold ground and clear towns | Artillery and engineers |
| Tanks | Breakthrough and exploitation | Infantry support and scouts |
| Engineers | Clear obstacles, fortify positions | Infantry and anti-tank guns |
| Anti-tank guns | Stop armored pushes | Defense in depth |
| Artillery | Prepare attacks, suppress defenders | Scouts for targeting |
| Scouts | Reconnaissance, finding gaps | Fast-moving formations |
| Headquarters | Coordination and order flow | Everything above |

| Officer action | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Hire an officer | Fills a division command slot and shapes how orders are executed |
| Fire an officer | Removes poor performance or a damaging quirk |
| Keep officers alive | Veterancy compounds across a long campaign |
| Heed advice | Sometimes reveals an opportunity you missed |
| Ignore advice | Sometimes the right call — commanders are not always right |

A common beginner mistake is treating officers as disposable. Community discussion suggests the opposite: a veteran commander who has survived several missions is worth more than a fresh replacement with better stats on paper.

## Logistics, Terrain, and the Real-World Battlefield

Logistics is where Battleplan WW2 separates itself from most World War II strategy games. Supply is simulated realistically, and terrain dictates how it flows. Cut an enemy's supply lines and their advance starves. Capture depots and your own offensive keeps rolling. Run low, and the correct answer is often to pull back rather than push on.

The maps themselves are built from real-world geographic data covering thousands of square kilometers. Roads, towns, forests, and swamps sit exactly where they do in real life, and elevation maps let you place artillery on genuine high ground rather than guessed-at ridgelines.

| Terrain feature | Movement effect | Supply effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roads | Fast movement | High throughput; the backbone of any advance |
| Towns | Slower, close-quarters fighting | Frequently the site of depots |
| Forests | Slow, with concealment | Restricted flow |
| Swamps | Very slow | Poor; a natural flank guard |
| High ground | Slower approach | Better observation for artillery |

The game also includes a history component: geo-tagged historical photos and videos appear where they were actually taken, so you can examine the real ground you are fighting over. For players who came to the category out of an interest in history rather than pure competition, this is one of the more thoughtful features in the package.

Practical tips for your first campaign:

- **Start defensive.** Defense in depth teaches you how orders propagate down the chain of command without costing you a division.
- **Draw in phases, not arrows.** Break every offensive into bombardment, breach, and exploitation.
- **Watch supply before the frontline.** A stalled advance is usually a logistics problem, not a firepower problem.
- **Put artillery on real elevation.** The map data is accurate; use it.
- **Protect your officers.** Veterancy and quirks accumulate only if they survive.
- **Use the time controls.** Missions last a week to a month — pause, play in real time, or fast-forward at will.
- **Expect a learning curve.** Community reports suggest the systems are deep, and depth takes time to absorb.

## Frequently Asked Questions About Battleplan WW2

**Is Battleplan WW2 real-time or turn-based?**
It is real-time, but with full time manipulation. Each mission is simulated second by second, and you can pause, play at normal speed, or fast-forward whenever you like. Missions range from one week to a month of in-game time, so the pacing is closer to a slow operational simulation than a fast tactical game.

**How long does a single mission take?**
According to the developer, mission lengths range from one week to one month of simulated time. Because you control the clock, a mission can be played in short sessions or fast-forwarded through quieter stretches.

**Can you play Battleplan WW2 single-player only?**
Yes. The Steam listing lists single-player as the headline feature, alongside 53 achievements, Steam Cloud support, and Family Sharing. There is no mention of a multiplayer mode on the store page.

**Is Battleplan WW2 historically accurate?**
The game leans heavily into authenticity. Battlefields are modeled from real-world geographic data, units are organized into realistic division structures with headquarters, regiments, battalions, and companies, and geo-tagged historical photos and videos let you compare the real locations with the ground you are fighting over. That said, it is a game first — your decisions will produce outcomes history never saw.
