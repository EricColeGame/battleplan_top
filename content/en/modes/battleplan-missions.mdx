## What Are Battleplan Missions?

Battleplan missions are the beating heart of Foolish Mortals Games' WW2 real-time strategy title, published by Slitherine Ltd. and released on October 12, 2026. Instead of compressing a battle into a twenty-minute skirmish, each Battleplan mission simulates a real engagement second by second — sometimes across a full week, sometimes across an entire month — while hundreds of thousands of troops move, fight, and resupply beneath your orders.

That scale is what separates the game from most WW2 strategy titles. You are not a squad leader micro-managing individual riflemen. You are a divisional commander who draws a plan directly onto the map, then watches your officers and companies carry it out autonomously. Your job is to think in days, not seconds, and to accept that a good plan executed on time usually beats a perfect plan executed late.

Early reception has been mixed but clearly engaged. At the time of writing, the game sits at a "Mixed" overall rating on Steam, with roughly two-thirds of 63 user reviews positive. Community reports suggest opinions are split in the way deep simulation wargames often are — some players love the ambition, others bounce off the learning curve.

## The Anatomy of a Battleplan Mission

Most Battleplan missions follow a recognizable rhythm, even though the terrain and objectives change dramatically from one operation to the next. Understanding that rhythm is the fastest way to stop losing divisions you spent an hour building.

| Phase | What You Do | What Happens on the Map |
|---|---|---|
| Reconnaissance and setup | Study elevation maps, roads, towns, forests, and swamps | Nothing moves; the battlefield is frozen while you plan |
| Preparatory bombardment | Assign artillery to soften a chosen sector | Defenders in the targeted area take losses and lose cohesion |
| Set-piece assault | Draw the main attack axis and assign divisions | Infantry, tanks, and engineers advance along your drawn route |
| Exploitation | Split the division to capture multiple objectives at once | Sub-units fan out autonomously toward each marked objective |
| Consolidation | Set defence in depth, planned withdrawals, counterattacks | Units dig in, fall back on schedule, or counter-punch |

The key insight is that you are never issuing individual move orders during the assault. Once the plan is drawn, your officers interpret it. That means the quality of your planning phase — not your clicking speed — decides the outcome.

### Time Controls and Mission Length

One of the most important features of Battleplan missions is that they never force you to react in real time unless you want to. Every operation can be paused, played at normal speed, or fast-forwarded.

| Control | When to Use It | Practical Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pause | Planning, reacting to a breakthrough, rewriting orders | Simulation freezes completely; you can redraw plans |
| Real-time | Watching a critical assault unfold | Troops move at a natural pace; good for reading the front |
| Fast-forward | Waiting out bombardments or supply buildup | Simulated hours pass in seconds |

Because a mission can run from one week to a month of in-game time, fast-forward is not a convenience — it is a necessity. The practical habit most players develop is to plan paused, run the opening minutes in real time, then fast-forward through the quiet stretches and pause again the moment something goes wrong.

## Drawing Orders: How Plans Flow Through the Chain of Command

The signature mechanic of the game is drawing. Rather than clicking waypoints one at a time, you sketch multi-step, multi-unit plans straight onto the map — arrows for advances, lines for defensive belts, markers for objectives. It is closer to annotating a paper map than to traditional RTS control.

### The Chain of Command

Each division is modeled with a realistic hierarchy, and you can customize the entire order of battle for your army.

| Level | What It Represents | What You Can Change |
|---|---|---|
| Division | Your primary maneuver unit | Composition, attached support, commander |
| Corps | Multiple divisions grouped for a big push | Which divisions combine and who leads |
| Regiment | Mid-level maneuver element | Attachments and task organization |
| Battalion | Core combat building block | Requisition and upgrades over the campaign |
| Company | Smallest splittable element | Detachments for raids and holding actions |

You can combine divisions into corps for a major offensive, or break units all the way down to companies when you need to grab several small objectives at once.

## Logistics, Supply, and Terrain in Battleplan Missions

Supply is not a background number — it is a weapon. Cutting an enemy's supply line starves them. Capturing supply depots fuels your own advance. Pulling back when your units run low is often the correct, if unglamorous, decision.

Supply flow is dictated by terrain, which means the map itself becomes a tactical puzzle. A single road through a swamp can feed an entire corps, and losing it can halt an offensive without a shot being fired. Roads, towns, forests, and even elevation are placed according to real-world geographic data, so the ground you are fighting over matches the ground soldiers actually fought over.

### Officers: Veterancy, Quirks, and Advice

| Officer Factor | What It Does | How to Handle It |
|---|---|---|
| Veterancy | Improves performance the longer an officer survives | Protect experienced commanders; do not waste them |
| Quirks | Adds unpredictable personality-driven behavior | Decide case by case whether the upside is worth it |
| Advice | Officers suggest courses of action during a mission | Listen when it fits your plan; ignore it when it does not |
| Hiring and firing | You choose who leads each division | Replace commanders who consistently underperform |

Keeping officers alive matters beyond the current mission. A veteran commander with a strong record is a long-term asset across an entire campaign, and the quirks they pick up along the way make each run feel distinct.

## Practical Tips for Winning Battleplan Missions

| Tip | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| Plan paused, always | Redrawing under real-time pressure is how offensives collapse |
| Bombard before you assault | Preparatory fire reduces defender cohesion on the chosen axis |
| Use defence in depth | A single thin line breaks; layered belts trade space for time |
| Plan withdrawals in advance | A planned pullback preserves units; a rout destroys them |
| Watch supply before objectives | A captured town you cannot supply is a liability |
| Split late, not early | Detaching companies too soon weakens your main effort |
| Keep scouts alive | Reconnaissance is what turns a guess into a plan |

A few additional habits pay off over a full campaign:

- **Study elevation before placing artillery.** High ground extends reach and improves survivability.
- **Requisition and upgrade steadily.** Units improve throughout the campaign, and a veteran formation outperforms a fresh one.
- **Treat fast-forward as a risk.** It is efficient, but breakthroughs can develop while you are skipping hours.
- **Do not chase every objective.** Splitting the division is powerful, but only when the objectives actually matter.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does a single Battleplan mission take?**
In-game, missions range from roughly one week to a full month of simulated time. In real-world hours that depends entirely on how much you pause and fast-forward — a careful player who plans extensively may spend far longer than someone who lets the simulation run.

**Can you pause during Battleplan missions?**
Yes. Every mission can be paused, played in real time, or fast-forwarded at will. Pausing is where the game is really played, because it is the only time you can redraw plans and reassign units without the clock moving.

**Are Battleplan missions historically grounded?**
The developers emphasize realism and simulation. Battlefields are built from real-world geographic data, so roads, towns, forests, swamps, and elevation match their real locations, and the game includes geo-tagged historical photos and videos placed where they were originally taken.

**Is there multiplayer in Battleplan missions?**
The game is presented as a single-player experience, with single-player features, Steam achievements, Cloud saves, and Family Sharing listed on its store page. If you are looking for competitive play, check the official store listing for the latest on any modes added after launch.

**What is the hardest part of Battleplan missions for new players?**
Almost universally, it is logistics and tempo. New commanders tend to over-commit, ignore supply lines, and split their divisions too early. Learning to pause, plan, and accept a deliberate withdrawal is what turns a losing mission into a winnable one.
