## What Battleplan Actually Is

Battleplan is a historical WWII real-time strategy game built around a simulation-first philosophy. Rather than clicking individual soldiers, you issue high-level orders and watch your officers and companies carry them out on their own. The result is a game that feels closer to a command simulator than a traditional action RTS.

The headline mechanic is drawing directly on the map. You sketch multi-step, multi-unit plans — a preparatory bombardment, a set-piece assault, a planned withdrawal — and your units execute them. It's an unusual approach that sets Battleplan apart from most WWII strategy titles on the market.

| Feature | How It Works |
| --- | --- |
| Draw-on-map planning | Create multi-stage, multi-unit orders by sketching directly on the battlefield |
| Realistic order of battle | Divisions modeled with HQ, regiments, battalions, and companies |
| Real-world terrain | Thousands of kilometers built from geographic data; roads, towns, forests, and swamps match real locations |
| Real-time simulation | Missions run second by second with pause, real-time, and fast-forward options |
| Logistics system | Cut enemy supply lines, capture depots, and pull back when supplies run low |
| Officer management | Hire and fire officers, keep them alive to gain veterancy and quirks |
| Historical media | Geo-tagged photos and videos placed where they were originally taken |

Mission lengths range from roughly one week to a full month of in-game time, which means a single scenario can be a serious commitment. You can pause at will, so the pacing is yours to control.

## Tips for Your First Battleplan Campaign

The learning curve here is real, but a few habits will make your opening hours much smoother.

- **Use the pause button aggressively.** The simulation runs second by second, so pausing to redraw plans costs you nothing and saves you from reactive mistakes.
- **Plan in stages, not steps.** Battleplan rewards multi-stage orders — bombardment, assault, then a split to capture several objectives at once.
- **Protect your officers.** They gain veterancy when they survive, and veteran officers with useful quirks are far more valuable than fresh replacements.
- **Watch supply before you push.** Cutting enemy supply lines can starve a defense faster than any frontal attack, and capturing depots fuels your own advance.
- **Read the elevation.** Artillery placed on high ground using real-world elevation data performs noticeably better than guns parked in a valley.
- **Inspect the historical media.** Geo-tagged photos and videos appear where they were actually taken, which makes the maps easier to learn and the history easier to remember.

If you're new to the genre entirely, consider playing your first mission on the slowest speed and treating it as a tutorial rather than a test.
