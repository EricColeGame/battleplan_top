## Why the Battleplan Trailer Matters More Than Most Game Trailers

Battleplan launched on October 12, 2026, and the Battleplan trailer is the fastest way to understand what makes this WW2 real-time strategy game different. Published by Slitherine and developed by Foolish Mortals Games, Battleplan simulates month-long battles involving hundreds of thousands of troops — and it does so without asking you to click every rifleman. The Battleplan trailer leans hard into that difference by showing the command layer itself: you draw orders onto a map, then watch your officers carry them out.

At $29.99, this is not an impulse buy for everyone. A two-minute trailer cannot answer every question about balance, performance, or long-term replay value. ## Drawing Orders: How the Core Loop Actually Works

The drawing tool is the heart of the game, and it is the single element the Battleplan trailer returns to again and again. Instead of selecting a unit and right-clicking a destination, you sketch the shape of the battle. A typical opening offensive might look like this:

1. **Study the ground.** Check real-world elevation maps before committing, because high ground still wins artillery duels.
2. **Draw the preparation.** Mark a preparatory bombardment on the enemy's forward positions.
3. **Sketch the main assault.** Draw the axis of advance for your lead regiment.
4. **Split the objective.** Divide the division so multiple towns or crossroads fall in the same phase.
5. **Set a fallback line.** Plan the withdrawal *before* you need it, not after.
6. **Hand it off.** Let officers and companies execute while you monitor supply and adjust.

| Plan type | What it looks like on the map | When to reach for it |
|---|---|---|
| Defence in depth | Layered lines with a thin forward screen | You expect to be pushed back |
| Counterattack | A staged arrow striking a flank | The enemy overextends |
| Planned withdrawal | A rearward line with a delay zone | Your units are low on supply |
| Preparatory bombardment | A marked barrage zone before the assault | You need to soften a strongpoint |
| Multi-objective split | Two or more converging arrows | Objectives are close together |

Because units act autonomously once orders are issued, your real job shifts to anticipation: reading supply, terrain, and officer quality rather than reacting to every contact.

## Army, Maps, and Logistics: The Realism Behind the Battleplan Trailer

The trailer's calm surface hides a dense simulation. Every system feeds the next, which is why the game can simulate a month of fighting second by second.

| System | How it works | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Chain of command | Divisions contain HQ, regiments, battalions, companies | Combine into corps or split down to companies |
| Officer careers | Hire, fire, and keep officers alive | Veterans gain experience — and quirks |
| Supply | Terrain dictates how supply flows | Cutting lines starves the enemy |
| Terrain | Built from real-world geographic data | Every road, town, and swamp is where it truly was |
| Time | Pause, real-time, or fast-forward at will | Missions last a week to a month |
| History | Geo-tagged photos and videos on location | Learn the ground and the events together |

The logistics layer deserves special attention, because it is the system most likely to decide whether your beautiful drawn plan survives contact. A plan that ignores supply depots will stall out mid-advance, and no amount of clever arrow-drawing fixes that. Player experience reports consistently point to supply as the skill ceiling in this style of wargame.
